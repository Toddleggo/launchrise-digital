# Resources {.backmatter #resources}

::: {.resources}
Tools and websites change quickly, so where possible I've listed organisations rather than products. Make sure any site you visit is the genuine one: type the address yourself or search for the organisation's name.

## Staying informed, calmly

Stanford University's annual *AI Index Report* (hai.stanford.edu) is the best single overview of where AI stands. The *International AI Safety Report* (internationalaisafetyreport.org) is an independent scientific review of capabilities and risks. METR (metr.org) publishes independent measurements of what AI systems can do. For AI and jobs, follow the Stanford Digital Economy Lab (digitaleconomy.stanford.edu), the Budget Lab at Yale (budgetlab.yale.edu), the Anthropic Economic Index (anthropic.com/economic-index) and the World Economic Forum's *Future of Jobs Report* (weforum.org). Pew Research Center (pewresearch.org) tracks public attitudes to AI.

## Learning to use AI

The help and learning sections of the major AI providers, including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Microsoft, have beginner guides and advice on writing good prompts. Many universities and learning platforms offer free introductory courses on AI; check reviews before you sign up for anything. Your local library, TAFE or community college may run AI workshops for adults and seniors.

## Scams and online safety

In Australia: Scamwatch (scamwatch.gov.au), ReportCyber through the Australian Cyber Security Centre (cyber.gov.au), and IDCARE (idcare.org) for free help if your identity has been compromised. In New Zealand: Netsafe and CERT NZ. In the United States: the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (ic3.gov) and the Federal Trade Commission (reportfraud.ftc.gov). In the United Kingdom: Action Fraud and the National Cyber Security Centre (ncsc.gov.uk). In Canada: the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre. Elsewhere, search for your national fraud reporting service and cyber security agency.

## Children and families

In Australia, the eSafety Commissioner (esafety.gov.au) has practical guides for parents on apps, AI companions, image-based abuse and the social media minimum age. Common Sense Media (commonsensemedia.org) publishes research and reviews of apps and AI tools. If a child is being threatened with images, report it to police; in Australia you can also report to the Australian Centre to Counter Child Exploitation (accce.gov.au), and in the United States to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children, whose Take It Down service can help remove images.

## Money

ASIC's Moneysmart (moneysmart.gov.au) in Australia, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau in the United States and MoneyHelper in the United Kingdom offer free, independent financial education. If you're struggling with debt in Australia, the National Debt Helpline (1800 007 007, ndh.org.au) offers free, confidential financial counselling; most countries have a similar service.

## Mental health and crisis support

If you or someone you know is in crisis, contact emergency services or a crisis line. In Australia: Lifeline, 13 11 14; Kids Helpline, 1800 55 1800; Beyond Blue, 1300 22 4636. In the United States: call or text 988. In the United Kingdom and Ireland: Samaritans, 116 123.

## Careers and trades

In Australia, Your Career (yourcareer.gov.au) and the Australian Apprenticeships website have information on apprenticeships and career changes. Professional bodies in most fields now publish guidance on AI for their members.
