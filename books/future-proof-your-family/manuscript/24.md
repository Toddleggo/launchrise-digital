# The AI Resilience Scorecard and a Last Word {.chapter number="24"}

You can't improve what you don't measure. This scorecard gives you a snapshot of how prepared you and your household are across the ten areas that matter most. Take it now, take it again after the 30-Day Challenge, and then take it every six months.

The score isn't a judgement of you as a person. It's a map. It shows you where you're strong and where to put your effort next.

## The AI Resilience Scorecard

For each area, give yourself a score from 0 to 5 using the descriptions. Be honest. Nobody's watching.

| 1. Income resilience | How secure is your household income? |
|---|---|
| 0 | One income, in a very high-exposure job (Automate share above 60 per cent), and no other sources. |
| 1 | One income, moderate to high exposure, no other sources. |
| 2 | One main income with moderate exposure, or two incomes that are both highly exposed. |
| 3 | A main income with low to moderate exposure, or a small second income under way. |
| 4 | A main income in a role with strong moats, plus a working second income. |
| 5 | Income from two or more sources, with your main role built on Anchor tasks. |

| 2. AI literacy | How well can you use AI? |
|---|---|
| 0 | I've never really used it. |
| 1 | I've tried it a few times and I'm not sure what to do with it. |
| 2 | I use it now and then for simple tasks. |
| 3 | I use it regularly, brief it well and check what matters. |
| 4 | I use it daily at work, I've set up at least one automation, and I help others. |
| 5 | I direct AI confidently across many tasks, use agents safely, and could teach others. |

| 3. Transferable skills | How portable are your skills if your role changed? |
|---|---|
| 0 | My skills are very specific to one highly exposed job. |
| 1 | I have some general skills but haven't thought about where they'd transfer. |
| 2 | I've identified my transferable skills and at least one next door. |
| 3 | I've started building skills for a next door. |
| 4 | I have a proof project or real experience in a second area. |
| 5 | I could move into at least two different roles within a few months if I had to. |

| 4. Financial resilience | How long could you cope with a drop in income? |
|---|---|
| 0 | No savings, high-interest debt, and fixed costs above 70 per cent of income. |
| 1 | Less than a month of expenses saved, and some high-interest debt. |
| 2 | About a month saved, and working on debt. |
| 3 | Two to three months saved and no high-interest debt. |
| 4 | Three to six months saved, and a written household downturn plan. |
| 5 | Six months or more saved, low fixed costs, a downturn plan and appropriate insurance. |

| 5. Digital security | How well protected are you against scams and cybercrime? |
|---|---|
| 0 | Same password everywhere, no multi-factor authentication, no family plan. |
| 1 | Some different passwords, no multi-factor authentication. |
| 2 | Multi-factor authentication on email or bank, and I know the scam red flags. |
| 3 | Multi-factor authentication on key accounts, a password manager, and a family safe word. |
| 4 | All of that, plus "hang up, call back" and the 24-hour rule agreed with family. |
| 5 | All of that, plus parents and vulnerable relatives protected, backups in place, and regular check-ins. |

| 4 | I'm known at work as someone who's adapting well, and I have a strong network. |

| 7. Earning opportunities | Are you positioned to benefit, not just survive? |

| 4 | I have a small but real second income or business. |
| 5 | I have a growing income stream or business built on AI-enabled or human-premium work. |

| 8. Family preparedness | Is your household ready? |

| 4 | The first three sections of the plan are done, and our downturn plan is written. |

| 10. Learning ability | How well do you keep learning? |
|---|---|

| 2 | I've started the 30-Day Challenge. |
| 3 | I've finished the 30-Day Challenge. |
| 4 | I learn something new every week and use AI as a tutor. |
| 5 | Learning is a habit. I pick up new skills quickly and teach others. |

## Your total

Add up your scores. If you skipped area 9, multiply your total by 50, then divide by 45, so your score is still out of 50.

| Score | What it means |
|---|---|
| 0–15: Exposed | You're where most people are. That isn't a criticism; it's the starting line. Begin with digital security and financial resilience, because they're the quickest to fix and protect you most. Then start the 30-Day Challenge. |
| 16–25: Waking up | You've started and you know the risks. Now it's about consistency. Pick your two lowest scores and put a plan against each. |
| 26–35: Adapting | You're well ahead of most people. Focus on building your second income and moving from AI user to AI director. |
| 36–45: Resilient | You're in a strong position for most versions of the future. Help others: your family, your team, your community. Teaching deepens your own skills. |
| 46–50: Future-ready | Genuinely impressive. Keep going, check the warning signs every quarter, and keep your moats strong. |

## A Last Word {.lastword}

I want to finish where I started.

Two years ago, I didn't know much about any of this. I just had a feeling in my bones that something big was happening and that I couldn't afford to look away. I've spent a lot of hours since then reading, testing, building, getting things wrong and slowly getting less wrong. And the more I learned, the less the question became "what is this technology?" and the more it became "who's going to tell the people I care about?"

That's what this book is. It's me telling you.

Here's what I believe after all of it.

Nobody knows exactly what the next five years hold. Not me, not the people building these systems, not the experts on television. Anyone who tells you precisely what 2031 looks like is guessing, and plenty of them are selling something.

But you've now seen what *is* known. The pay slips showing young people quietly locked out of jobs that used to be their way in. The Doubling Clock. The scam figures. The tutoring studies that show what AI can do for a child's learning when it's used well, and the research hinting at what it costs when it isn't. Driverless cars carrying paying passengers. Electricians in demand because of buildings full of computers. The three scenarios, and the warning signs for each. You know more about what's actually going on than most of the people around you.

And more importantly, you've got a plan.

A safe word that could stop someone you love from losing their savings to a voice that sounds exactly like yours. A Task Test that shows you where your job is strong and where it's exposed. A buffer, even a small one, that buys you time. A second income, even a tiny one, so you're never standing on one leg. Skills most people won't have for years. Kids who learn to think with AI instead of letting it think for them. A family that talks about this together instead of worrying about it alone.

None of that depends on knowing which future turns up. All of it makes you stronger in every one of them.

I've spent a lot of this book on what could go wrong, because you deserve honesty and because the risks are real. But I want to leave you with the other side, because it's just as real.

We may be entering a time when one person with the right skills can do what used to take a team. When a kid in a village with a phone can get tutoring that used to be available only to the rich. When a tradie gets their evenings back. When a nurse spends more time with patients and less with paperwork. When someone with a good idea and no money can build a business from the kitchen table. When a person who lost their job at fifty discovers, to their own surprise, that twenty-five years of experience is worth *more* in a world full of machines that have read everything and lived nothing.

Some of that is already happening. More of it will happen, and mostly for people who are ready.

AI isn't something you need to worship, and it isn't something you need to fear. It's a tool, an extraordinary one, arriving faster than any tool before it. But ignoring it is a choice too. It just happens to be the choice that leaves the decisions to everyone else.

You picked up this book because some part of you sensed that something big was shifting and you didn't want to be caught standing still. Trust that instinct. It's the same one that has kept people safe through every great change in history: pay attention, get ready, look after your people.

You can't control how fast AI moves. You can control how ready you are when it arrives. And by reading this far, you're already more ready than you were.

So learn. Adapt. Protect your family. Build some options. Help your kids become capable, curious, decent people who can think for themselves. And start now, before it becomes obvious.

Then, if this book helped, give it to someone you love.

*Todd Lego*
