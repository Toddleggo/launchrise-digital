# The Family AI Preparedness Plan {.chapter number="18"}

Most of this book asks you to think. This chapter asks you to do.

It pulls the most important family actions from across the book into one checklist, in the order I'd tackle them. Copy it, print it, stick it on the fridge. Don't try to do it all in one weekend. Do the "this week" section first, because it's the most urgent and it's quick, then work your way down.

One tip: do this together, with your partner, with your kids if they're old enough, and with your parents if you can. The conversations you have while filling it in are half the value.

## This week: safety first

### Scam protection (Chapter 16)

::: {.checklist}
- We have chosen a family safe word.
- Everyone who needs it has been told, in person or on a call we made.
- Our parents and older relatives know it and understand why.
- A "Hang up, call back" card with our bank's real number is by the phone or on the fridge.
- We've agreed the 24-hour rule for unexpected transfers over [ ]{.blank}.
- Everyone knows never to pay with gift cards, crypto or wire transfers to someone who contacted *us*.
- Everyone knows caller ID can be faked.
:::

### Account security

::: {.checklist}
- Multi-factor authentication is on for email, banking, social media and our phone accounts.
- We use a password manager, or at least different passwords for important accounts.
- We've asked our bank about transfer limits, alerts and a trusted contact.
:::

### The kids' immediate safety (Chapters 17 and 19)

::: {.checklist}
- We've checked which AI apps and chatbots our kids use.
- We've checked age ratings and parental controls on those apps.
- We've agreed that phones stay out of bedrooms at night.
- The kids have heard us say: "If anyone threatens you with pictures, real or fake, tell us straight away. You will never be in trouble."
:::

## This year: skills and income

::: {.checklist}
- Each adult has finished the 30-Day Challenge and uses AI for at least one real task a day.
- Each adult has updated their résumé or portfolio with specific, AI-related results.
- First leg of the tripod: we've each taken a step to strengthen our main income (become the AI person at work, moved towards Anchor tasks, built our network).
- Second leg: we've chosen one earning path from Chapter 13 and earned our first dollar from it.
- Third leg: our buffer has reached the next rung on the ladder.
- All high-interest debt is listed, with a plan and a target date to clear it.
:::

## The kids (Chapter 19)

For each child, write their name and age, then work through the list.

::: {.checklist}
- We've talked about what AI is and isn't: not a person, and sometimes wrong.
- We've agreed rules for AI and schoolwork: use it to learn, not to skip the thinking.
- They know the family safe word, if they're old enough.
- They know the three scam red flags: urgency, secrecy and unusual payment.
- We know which apps and AI tools they use.
- We've talked about fake images and videos.
- They're building at least one hands-on skill: cooking, building, sport, art, music, gardening.
- They're building at least one people skill: a team, a club, volunteering or a part-time job.
- For teenagers: we've talked honestly about future careers and what's changing.
:::

## Our parents and older relatives

For each person, work through the list.

::: {.checklist}
- They know the safe word.
- They have the "Hang up, call back" card.
- Multi-factor authentication is set up on their email and bank, with our help if needed.
- Their bank has been asked about a trusted contact and transfer limits.
- We check in with them regularly, and we've agreed how often.
- They know they can ring us about anything suspicious without feeling silly.
- We've shown them one useful, safe way to use AI, such as asking questions, reading small print or translating a sign with the camera.
:::

## The family conversation

Once a year, maybe around New Year or a birthday, sit down as a family for half an hour and talk through a handful of questions. What's changed this year in our jobs, the news and the tools we use? Which of the three scenarios from Chapter 5 looks most like where we're heading, and have we seen any warning signs? How's our tripod, and which leg is weakest? How are the kids going with AI, at school and socially? Has anyone had a scam attempt, and what happened? What's one thing each of us wants to learn next year? And what are we grateful for? Seriously. It helps.

Write the answers down and keep them. In five years, reading back through them will be fascinating, and you'll see how much further ahead you were than you felt at the time.

## Every six months

::: {.checklist}
- Does everyone still know the safe word? Change it if it's been shared too widely.
- Are the security settings still on?
- Is the buffer growing?
- Have we redone our Task Tests?
- Have we retaken the AI Resilience Scorecard (Chapter 24)?
- Have we checked the kids' apps and rules?
- Have we checked in on our parents?
:::

## A final word on the plan

You won't tick every box straight away. No family does. But every box you tick makes your household a little harder to rob, a little harder to knock over, and a little better prepared for whatever the next five years bring.

And there's something else. Working through this together, talking about money, safety, work and the future with the people you love, builds something no technology can give you and none can take away: a family that faces change together rather than alone. That's the real preparedness.
