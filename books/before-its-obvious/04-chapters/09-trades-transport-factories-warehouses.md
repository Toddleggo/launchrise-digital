# Chapter 9
## Trades, Transport, Factories & Warehouses

If you work with your hands, you've probably spent the last few years hearing about AI and thinking: *good luck getting a chatbot to fix a burst pipe at 2am.*

You're right.

And for most of this book's five-year window, you're going to stay right. But there's more to this chapter than "you're safe." There are three stories here — one about robots, one about a boom that's happening *because* of AI, and one about the biggest opportunity sitting right in front of tradespeople that most of them are ignoring.

---

### Story one: the robots are real, and they're slower than the videos

You've seen the videos. Humanoid robots doing backflips. Robot hands folding laundry. Robot dogs climbing stairs. Sleek machines walking through factories.

Here's what's actually going on, as of 2026.

**Humanoid robots** — the ones shaped like people — are in real trials in factories and warehouses. Companies like Figure and Agility Robotics have robots doing paid work for customers: Figure's robots have been loading sheet-metal parts at a BMW plant in South Carolina, and Agility's Digit robots have been moving boxes and totes in warehouses. But as of mid-2026, independent trackers of the industry reported that no humanoid robot maker had sustained commercial deployments beyond the low hundreds of units. Tesla has built many of its Optimus robots, but Elon Musk himself described them in early 2026 as being mainly for learning and data collection rather than productive work.

**In homes,** robots are far behind. The Stanford AI Index 2026 found robots succeeded at only around **12% of household tasks** they were tested on.

Why is the physical world so hard? Because it's messy. A factory can be designed around a robot: same parts, same place, same lighting, every time. A house built in 1962 with a crawl space full of spider webs, corroded fittings, a wall that isn't quite straight and a dog barking at you is a completely different problem. Human hands, eyes and judgement handle that kind of chaos without thinking. Machines, so far, can't.

> **FORECAST LABEL: NOW** — Humanoid robots are in early commercial trials; deployments are small.
> **POSSIBLE** — Robots doing meaningful amounts of paid work in structured environments (warehouses, factories) at real scale by 2029–2031.
> **WILD CARD** — General-purpose robots doing skilled trades work in homes and on building sites within this five-year window.

---

### Driving is different — it's already happening

One area where machines really *are* doing the physical job: driving.

Waymo, the self-driving company owned by Google's parent Alphabet, was providing around **500,000 paid rides per week** across American cities by early-to-mid 2026 — ten times what it was doing two years earlier — with no one in the driver's seat. It was expanding city by city through 2026 and aiming for a million rides a week by the end of the year.

In trucking, Aurora Innovation began running driverless heavy trucks commercially on Texas highways in 2025, first between Dallas and Houston, then on longer routes, and was aiming for hundreds of driverless trucks on the road by the end of 2026.

This is the clearest example of AI taking over a physical job. And it's worth understanding *how* it's happening: **city by city, route by route, in good weather first, on well-mapped roads first.** Not everywhere at once.

> **FORECAST LABEL: NOW** — Driverless taxis and some driverless trucking are operating commercially in parts of the US.
> **LIKELY** — Continued expansion to more cities and highway corridors through 2031.
> **POSSIBLE** — Significant reduction in demand for taxi, rideshare and long-haul drivers in certain regions by 2031.

## Role-by-role breakdowns

### Electricians

**AI touches:** Quoting, invoicing, scheduling, reading plans, fault-finding help (describe a problem, get likely causes), compliance paperwork, marketing.

**Protected:** Basically everything that involves being on site. Wiring, testing, diagnosing real faults in real buildings, safety sign-off.

**The bonus:** Electricians are one of the biggest *winners* of the AI boom. More on that below.

**Forecast: VERY LOW exposure. HIGH opportunity.**

### Plumbers, gasfitters, HVAC technicians

**AI touches:** Quoting, admin, customer communication, diagnosing from photos and descriptions, marketing.

**Protected:** The work itself. Every house is different. Every job is a puzzle solved with hands.

**The bonus:** HVAC and cooling are in massive demand from data centres.

**Forecast: VERY LOW exposure. HIGH opportunity for HVAC.**

### Builders, carpenters, roofers, bricklayers, tilers, painters

**AI touches:** Estimating, project planning, ordering materials, design visualisation for clients, admin, marketing.

**Protected:** The physical build.

**Watch:** Prefabrication and factory-built modular housing could grow, which moves some construction work into factories where automation is easier. That's a slow shift, not a sudden one.

**Forecast: LOW exposure.**

### Mechanics and automotive technicians

**AI touches:** Diagnostics (increasingly AI-assisted), parts ordering, customer communication, service records.

**Protected:** Physical repair.

**Watch:** Electric vehicles have fewer moving parts and different skills. Mechanics who get qualified on EVs and high-voltage systems are positioning themselves well.

**Forecast: LOW exposure. MODERATE change (EV transition).**

### Cleaners

**AI touches:** Scheduling, booking, quoting, marketing.

**Protected:** Most cleaning in homes and varied commercial spaces. Robot vacuums exist and are getting better, but the full range of cleaning a person does — bathrooms, kitchens, clutter, judgement about what matters to the client — remains very hard for robots.

**Watch:** Large, open, simple commercial spaces (warehouses, airports, shopping centres) are where robotic floor cleaning is growing.

**Forecast: LOW exposure (homes); MODERATE (large commercial floor areas).**

### Truck drivers

**What's happening:** Driverless trucks are operating on some US highway routes. This is real.

**Likely pattern:** Long, straight highway runs between big depots are automated first. Local delivery, city driving, complicated loading, bad weather, rural roads and specialist freight (dangerous goods, oversize loads, livestock) stay human much longer.

**Honest outlook:** If you're a long-haul highway driver, especially in the US Sun Belt, this is the most directly exposed trades-and-transport role in this chapter. It won't happen everywhere overnight — it'll spread route by route — but it's worth planning now.

**Transitions:** Local and specialist driving. Fleet coordination and logistics. Supervising autonomous trucks from terminals. Diesel and EV mechanics. Dispatching. Heavy equipment operation. Your knowledge of roads, loads and logistics is valuable — it needs a new home before it needs one urgently.

**Forecast: MODERATE-TO-HIGH exposure (long-haul highway); LOW-TO-MODERATE (local, specialist).**

### Taxi and rideshare drivers

**What's happening:** Robotaxis are operating commercially in a growing number of American cities.

**Honest outlook:** If you drive in a city where Waymo or a competitor is operating, watch closely. Elsewhere, you likely have more time — but the direction is clear.

**Transitions:** Specialist transport (patients, elderly, disability transport where care and assistance matter), delivery that involves more than dropping a package, logistics roles, or retraining into trades.

**Forecast: HIGH exposure in cities with robotaxis; MODERATE elsewhere by 2031.**

### Warehouse workers

**What's happening:** Warehouses are among the most automated workplaces already — conveyor systems, sorting machines, robots that carry shelves to workers. Humanoid and mobile robots are being trialled for picking and moving.

**Protected (for now):** Picking unusual items, handling damaged goods, problem-solving, maintenance, anything requiring flexibility.

**Outlook:** Continued gradual automation. The growth jobs are in **maintaining, fixing and supervising** the machines. Demand for robotics technicians has been rising sharply.

**Transitions:** Robotics and automation technician (often via a short course or apprenticeship). Maintenance. Logistics coordination.

**Forecast: MODERATE-TO-HIGH exposure.**

### Factory and manufacturing workers

**Outlook:** Manufacturing has been automating for decades; AI and robotics continue that. Repetitive, predictable work in controlled environments is most exposed. Skilled trades within manufacturing — maintenance fitters, electricians, toolmakers, technicians — remain in demand and often short-staffed.

**Forecast: MODERATE exposure (repetitive roles); LOW (skilled maintenance and technical roles).**

### Story two: the boom nobody expected

Here's the plot twist in the AI story that almost nobody saw coming.

Artificial intelligence runs on enormous buildings full of computers called **data centres**. Those buildings need electricity — a staggering amount of it. The International Energy Agency expects global data centre electricity use to roughly double by 2030, to about the amount Japan uses today.

Someone has to build those buildings. Wire them. Cool them. Build the power lines to them. Maintain the generators. Build the new power stations and grid connections.

That someone isn't a chatbot.

In a 2026 survey by the Associated General Contractors of America, **58% of construction firms** said data centre projects were increasing competition for workers, and nearly half said they felt pressure to raise wages. Reports through 2026 described data centre electricians in high-demand areas earning very high hourly rates, with overtime on top.

Put simply: AI is creating a gold rush, and the people selling the shovels are electricians, HVAC technicians, linesmen, construction workers, welders and engineers.

> **FORECAST LABEL: NOW** — AI infrastructure is driving strong demand for skilled trades in construction, electrical and cooling work.
> **LIKELY** — This continues through at least 2030, based on announced investment and energy projections.

For young people choosing a path, and for anyone thinking about retraining, this deserves serious attention. An electrical apprenticeship might be one of the most AI-proof, high-demand career choices available right now. I don't say that lightly.

### Story three: the opportunity tradies are ignoring

Here's the thing I most want tradespeople to hear.

AI is not coming for your *work*. But it's the best thing that's ever happened to your *business*.

Ask any self-employed tradie what they hate about their job, and I'd bet it's not the tools. It's:

- Writing quotes at 9pm.
- Chasing invoices.
- Answering the same phone questions twenty times a week.
- Trying to keep up with social media.
- Writing up job notes and compliance paperwork.
- Scheduling around cancellations.
- Doing the books.

AI can help with nearly every one of those, today.

- Talk a quote into your phone on the drive home; have AI turn it into a professional written quote.
- Take photos of a finished job; have AI write the Facebook post and the Google Business update.
- Set up an AI assistant to answer common customer questions and book jobs.
- Use AI to write up compliance notes from a voice memo.
- Use accounting software with AI features to match receipts and chase invoices.

Think about what that does. The tradie who gets their admin down from ten hours a week to three isn't just less stressed. They can take more jobs, respond faster, look more professional and win more work than the one down the road still writing quotes on the back of an envelope.

This is the lowest-hanging fruit in the entire book. If you're a tradesperson, Chapter 14 and the 30-day challenge in Chapter 15 will give you the practical how-to.

### The honest risk for trades

I don't want to give you a false sense of security. So here's the realistic risk for trades over this five-year window.

**It's not robots. It's other people.**

If AI disrupts a lot of office jobs, some of those workers will retrain into trades. That's more competition for apprenticeships and, eventually, for work. Meanwhile, if white-collar incomes are squeezed in a downturn, people may put off renovations and non-urgent jobs.

So the smart move for trades isn't to relax. It's to:

1. **Get the highest qualification you can** in your trade — licences and specialisations are moats.
2. **Specialise** in areas with growing demand: electrical, solar and batteries, EV charging, HVAC and cooling, data centre work, smart home systems.
3. **Use AI to run a tighter business** than your competitors.
4. **Build your reputation and reviews** — trust is a moat, and online reviews are how trust scales.
5. **Consider training others.** If people move into trades, someone has to train them.
