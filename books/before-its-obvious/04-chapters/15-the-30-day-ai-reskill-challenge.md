# Chapter 15
## The 30-Day AI Reskill Challenge

Reading about AI doesn't make you good at AI. Using it does.

So here's the deal. Thirty days. One task a day. Most take 15 to 30 minutes. A few take a bit longer. You don't need any technical skill to start, and you don't need to pay for anything (though a paid subscription to one AI tool will make some days easier).

By Day 30, you'll be more capable with AI than most people you know — including, probably, some who've been using it for years.

**How to do it:**
- Keep a simple log: a notebook or a note on your phone. Date, what you did, one thing you learned.
- If you miss a day, don't restart. Just pick up where you left off.
- Do it with someone if you can. A partner, a friend, a workmate, your teenager. It's more fun and you'll finish.
- Use real tasks from your real life wherever possible. That's what makes it stick.

---

## WEEK 1: FOUNDATIONS
*Goal: get comfortable, get curious, learn its strengths and weaknesses.*

**Day 1 — Say hello properly.**
Open a free AI assistant. Tell it about yourself — your job, your family situation (no private details), what you're hoping to get from AI. Ask: *"Based on what I've told you, what are ten ways you could help me in my daily life?"* Try one.

**Day 2 — The CRAFT test.**
Pick one real writing task (an email, a message, a post). Do it with a lazy four-word prompt. Then do it with CRAFT (Chapter 14). Compare. Write down the difference.

**Day 3 — Catch it lying.**
Ask it detailed questions about something you're an expert in — your job, your hometown, your hobby. Keep going until you find a mistake. Write down what it got wrong and how confident it sounded.

**Day 4 — Make it teach you.**
Pick something you've always wanted to understand (how mortgages work, how the stock market works, how to read a nutrition label, how electricity gets to your house). Use the tutor prompt from Chapter 14: *"Don't give me the answers. Teach me step by step and quiz me."*

**Day 5 — Show it the world.**
Use the camera. Photograph a plant, a form, a recipe, a broken appliance, a sign in another language, a document. Ask it to explain, translate or help.

**Day 6 — Talk to it.**
Use voice mode, if your tool has one. Have a spoken conversation while you walk, cook or drive (hands-free). Plan your week out loud.

**Day 7 — Privacy and reflection.**
Check your privacy settings. Then look back at your week's log. What surprised you? Where was it useful? Where was it useless? Write three sentences.

---

## WEEK 2: AI AT WORK
*Goal: make AI useful in your actual job.*

**Day 8 — The Task Test, AI-assisted.**
Redo your Task Test from Chapter 6, but this time, describe your job to the AI and ask it to help you list your tasks. Compare its list with yours. What did it add? What did it miss?

**Day 9 — Speed up an Assist task.**
Take one Assist task from your list. Do it with AI today, start to finish, including checking. Time yourself.

**Day 10 — Summarise something long.**
Take a long document, email thread, report or article relevant to your work (no confidential information unless you're using an approved work tool). Ask for a summary, key points, and "what should I do about this?"

**Day 11 — Prepare for a conversation.**
You've got a meeting, a customer call, a difficult conversation or an interview coming up. Ask AI to role-play the other person. Practise twice.

**Day 12 — Research properly.**
Research a real question for your work or life using AI — then apply the Two-Source Rule. Check every important claim against two reliable, non-AI sources. How many held up?

**Day 13 — The spreadsheet day.**
Take a spreadsheet (a budget, a sales list, anything without sensitive data). Ask AI to analyse it, find patterns, create a chart and write a formula you need.

**Day 14 — Teach someone.**
Show a colleague, friend or family member one thing you've learned so far. Watch their face. Teaching locks it in.

---

## WEEK 3: BUILD
*Goal: move from using AI to making things with it.*

**Day 15 — Your first automation.**
Pick one small repetitive task. Using a no-code automation tool (Chapter 14), set up one simple rule — for example, saving email attachments to a folder, or a weekly reminder. Or simply write down, step by step, what you'd want automated. Ask AI to help you set it up.

**Day 16 — Build a tiny app.**
Ask AI to build you a simple web page tool: a chore chart, a budget calculator, a quote calculator, a habit tracker. Open it. Use it. Ask for one improvement.

**Day 17 — Make something visual.**
Create an image, a poster, a simple logo or a presentation with AI. Then critique it: what's good, what's generic, what would a real designer do differently?

**Day 18 — Try an agent.**
Give an AI agent a multi-step, low-stakes research task: *"Find the five best-reviewed [service] in [area], compare prices and reviews, and give me a table."* Watch how it works. Note where it struggles.

**Day 19 — Write something bigger.**
Use AI as an interviewer to help you write something longer and meaningful: a one-page business idea, a family history story, a letter to your kids, a proposal for your boss. Let it ask the questions; you provide the substance.

**Day 20 — Your personal AI setup.**
Many AI tools let you set custom instructions or save information about yourself so you don't have to repeat it. Write yours: who you are, what you do, how you like answers (short? detailed? plain English?). Save it.

**Day 21 — Weekly review.**
Look at your log. Update your Task Test. What would you now mark differently? Which Assist tasks are becoming easy?

---

## WEEK 4: EARN, PROTECT & TEACH
*Goal: turn your new skill into security and opportunity.*

**Day 22 — Scam-proof your family.**
Set up a family safe word (Chapter 16). Call or message the people you'd share it with. Explain why in two sentences.

**Day 23 — Deepfake awareness.**
Search for examples of AI-generated images and voices online (reputable news coverage of deepfakes is a good start). Try spotting them. Then accept the lesson: you often can't tell by looking. That's why the safe word matters.

**Day 24 — Your one-sentence offer.**
Pick one earning path from Chapter 13. Write your offer: *"I help [who] with [what] so they can [result]."* Ask AI to critique it and improve it.

**Day 25 — Do one free piece of work.**
For someone you know — a friend's business, a relative, a local group — do one useful AI-assisted task for free. Ask for feedback.

**Day 26 — Update your proof.**
Update your CV, LinkedIn profile or portfolio with your new AI skills — with *specific examples*. Not "proficient in AI." But: "Used AI tools to cut weekly reporting time from 4 hours to 1."

**Day 27 — Talk to your boss (or your customers).**
If employed: suggest one way your team could use AI, based on what you've learned. If self-employed: tell a few customers about a new or faster service. If job-seeking: tell your network what you've been learning.

**Day 28 — Plan the next 90 days.**
Ask AI to help you build a 90-day learning plan for the "next door" you chose in Chapter 11, using mostly free resources.

**Day 29 — Teach a mini-class.**
Teach three people something useful about AI. Your parents, your kids, your team, your friends. Fifteen minutes. You'll realise how much you've learned.

**Day 30 — Celebrate and score yourself.**
Take the AI Resilience Scorecard in Chapter 24. Look back at Day 1 in your log. Notice how far you've come. Then write down your next three moves.

---

### What happens after Day 30

Keep a small daily habit: use AI for at least one real task every day. Keep your log weekly. Every three months, redo your Task Test and Scorecard.

The Doubling Clock keeps ticking. The tools will keep changing. But once you've done these thirty days, you won't be learning from scratch each time. You'll have the habits — briefing, checking, iterating, building, protecting — and those transfer to whatever comes next.

That's what being AI-native actually means. Not knowing every tool. Knowing how to learn the next one.
