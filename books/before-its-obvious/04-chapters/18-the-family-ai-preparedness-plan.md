# Chapter 18
## The Family AI Preparedness Plan

Most of this book asks you to think. This chapter asks you to *do*.

It's a checklist. You can photocopy it, print it, stick it on the fridge. It pulls together the most important family actions from across the book into one place, in the order I'd do them.

Don't try to do it all in one weekend. Do the **This Week** section first. It's the most urgent, and it's quick. Then work down.

A tip: do this *together*. With your partner, your kids if they're old enough, your parents if you can. The conversations you'll have while filling it in are half the value.

## SECTION 6: THE FAMILY CONVERSATION

Once a year — maybe around New Year, or a birthday — sit down as a family for half an hour and talk through these:

1. **What's changed this year?** In our jobs, in the news, in the tools we use.
2. **Which of the three futures** (Chapter 5) looks most like where we're heading? Any warning signs?
3. **How's our Tripod?** Which leg is weakest?
4. **How are the kids going** with AI — at school and socially?
5. **Has anyone had a scam attempt?** What happened?
6. **What's one thing each of us wants to learn next year?**
7. **What are we grateful for?** (Seriously. It helps.)

Write down the answers. Keep them. In five years, reading back through them will be fascinating — and you'll see how much further ahead you were than you felt at the time.

### A final word on this plan

You won't tick every box straight away. That's fine. No family does.

But every box you tick makes your household a little harder to rob, a little harder to knock over, and a little better prepared for whatever the next five years brings.

And there's something else. Doing this together — talking about money, safety, work and the future with the people you love — builds something that no technology can give you and none can take away: a family that faces change *together*, rather than alone.

That's the real preparedness.
