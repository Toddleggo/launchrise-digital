# Chapter 11
## If You Do X, Start Learning Y

This is the chapter I'd want to hand to someone who's just done the Task Test, got a high score, and is sitting at the kitchen table thinking: *okay, so now what?*

Here's the most important thing I can tell you about changing direction:

> **You are not starting from zero. You never are.**

Every job you've done has given you things that transfer. Knowledge of an industry. Understanding of customers. Skills with people. Problem-solving habits. Reliability. Things you know that you don't even realise you know, because you've known them so long.

The trick of a good career change isn't to throw all that away and start again. It's to find the *next door* — a role where your existing experience is an advantage, and you only need to add a few new skills to walk through it.

This chapter is a map of those doors.

---

### How to read the Reskill Map

For each current role, I'll give you:

- **Your transferable strengths** — what you already have.
- **Next doors** — roles where those strengths give you a head start.
- **Learn** — the specific skills to add.
- **First step** — something you could start this month.

These aren't the only options. They're starting points to get you thinking. And a few honest cautions:

- Some of these new roles are still emerging and job titles vary a lot between companies and countries.
- Pay and demand vary by where you live. Check job listings in your area before committing to anything expensive.
- **Be very careful with expensive courses.** There's a gold rush of "AI certifications" of wildly varying quality. Many of the best learning resources are free or cheap. (See the Resources section at the end of the book.)

## The Reskill Map

### OFFICE & ADMIN

**Receptionist / front desk**
- *Strengths:* People skills, calm under pressure, organisation, knowing how a business runs day to day.
- *Next doors:* Patient/client experience coordinator. Office operations coordinator. AI front-desk system manager (running the booking and phone AI, handling what it can't). Practice manager.
- *Learn:* AI booking and phone systems. Basic automation (Chapter 14). Customer experience principles. Basic project management.
- *First step:* Offer to lead your workplace's trial of an AI booking or phone tool.

**Data entry / admin assistant**
- *Strengths:* Accuracy, attention to detail, knowledge of business systems and processes.
- *Next doors:* AI operations / automation assistant. Data quality checker for AI systems. Operations coordinator. Bookkeeper → finance operations.
- *Learn:* Spreadsheet skills to an advanced level. No-code automation tools. How to check and correct AI output systematically.
- *First step:* Automate one repetitive task you do every week (Chapter 14 shows you how).

**Executive assistant**
- *Strengths:* Trust, discretion, juggling priorities, understanding how senior people think.
- *Next doors:* Chief of staff. Operations manager. Executive operations lead running a stack of AI tools for a leader or team.
- *Learn:* Project management. Business basics (budgets, strategy). Directing AI agents.
- *First step:* Ask your manager for one strategic project to own, not just support.

### FINANCE

**Bookkeeper**
- *Strengths:* Understanding of how money moves through small businesses; trust of clients.
- *Next doors:* Fractional CFO / business advisor for small businesses. AI finance systems specialist. Cash-flow coach.
- *Learn:* Management accounting and forecasting. Advisory and communication skills. The AI features of the main accounting platforms.
- *First step:* Offer three existing clients a monthly "numbers conversation" — 30 minutes explaining what their figures mean.

**Junior accountant / accounts clerk**
- *Strengths:* Technical knowledge, systems familiarity.
- *Next doors:* Tax advisory. Forensic accounting and fraud investigation (a growth area — see Chapter 16). Financial systems implementation. Audit of AI-driven processes.
- *Learn:* A specialism. Data analysis. Fraud and risk.
- *First step:* Pick one specialism and read its professional body's entry requirements.

**Insurance claims / loan processor**
- *Strengths:* Understanding of rules, risk and customer situations.
- *Next doors:* Complex claims handler. Fraud investigator. Customer advocate / hardship specialist. AI decision reviewer / compliance officer.
- *Learn:* Investigation skills. Compliance and regulation. Handling vulnerable customers.
- *First step:* Volunteer for complex or disputed cases.

### CUSTOMER-FACING

**Call centre / customer service agent**
- *Strengths:* Empathy, patience, problem-solving, product knowledge, handling difficult people.
- *Next doors:* Customer success manager. Complex case specialist. AI conversation designer / trainer (helping improve how AI assistants respond). Community manager. Sales.
- *Learn:* How AI chatbots are set up and improved. Customer success practices. Relationship management.
- *First step:* Keep a log of the questions the AI (or the script) handles badly, and why. Offer it to your team leader. That's the start of an AI trainer's job.

**Retail worker**
- *Strengths:* Talking to strangers, product knowledge, handling pressure, visual merchandising, stock.
- *Next doors:* Local business AI helper (setting up simple AI tools for small shops). E-commerce assistant. Customer experience roles. Store management.
- *Learn:* Online selling platforms. Social media for small business. Basic AI tools for business.
- *First step:* Help one local business set up their Google Business profile and AI-written product descriptions — free, as a portfolio piece.

**Salesperson (transactional / high-volume)**
- *Strengths:* Resilience, persuasion, reading people.
- *Next doors:* Relationship/consultative sales in complex products. Account management. Partnerships.
- *Learn:* Industry depth in one sector. Consultative selling.
- *First step:* Choose one industry to go deep on and start learning its language.

### CREATIVE & TECH

**Copywriter / content writer**
- *Strengths:* Writing, clarity, persuasion, research.
- *Next doors:* Content strategist. Editor. Brand voice lead. Specialist writer in a field you know deeply. Ghostwriter for experts. AI content quality lead.
- *Learn:* Strategy. A specialism. Building a personal audience.
- *First step:* Pick your specialism and publish one piece a week in it.

**Graphic designer**
- *Strengths:* Visual taste, tools, client work.
- *Next doors:* Art director. Brand designer. UX/UI designer. AI creative director.
- *Learn:* Brand strategy. User experience basics. Directing AI image tools.
- *First step:* Redo one old project as "art director": use AI for variations, you choose and refine. Document the process.

**Junior developer**
- *Strengths:* Technical fundamentals.
- *Next doors:* AI engineer (building with AI models). Developer in a specific domain (health, finance). Solutions engineer. Security. Automation specialist for businesses.
- *Learn:* AI coding agents. Building on AI models. One domain deeply. Security basics.
- *First step:* Build and publish one useful tool for a real person you know.

### HEALTH & EDUCATION

**Medical admin / billing / coding**
- *Strengths:* Healthcare systems knowledge, accuracy.
- *Next doors:* Patient navigator / care coordinator. Clinical informatics support. Practice manager. Clinical support roles (with training).
- *Learn:* Patient-facing skills. Healthcare IT systems. Short clinical or care qualifications.
- *First step:* Look up patient navigator or care coordinator roles near you and what they require.

**Teacher (considering leaving)**
- *Strengths:* Explaining things, managing groups, planning, patience, reading people.
- *Next doors:* Learning designer / instructional designer. Corporate trainer (especially AI training — every company needs it). AI education specialist. Education product roles.
- *Learn:* Instructional design. AI tools for learning. Workplace training methods.
- *First step:* Build one short AI-for-beginners workshop and run it for friends or a local group.

**Tutor**
- *Strengths:* One-on-one teaching, motivation.
- *Next doors:* AI-supported tutoring (serving more students using AI practice tools). Learning coach focused on accountability and study skills. Special-needs tutoring. Teaching families to use AI well.
- *Learn:* AI tutoring tools. Coaching methods.
- *First step:* Redesign your offer around what AI *can't* do: accountability, motivation, confidence.

### TRADES & TRANSPORT

**Long-haul truck driver**
- *Strengths:* Logistics knowledge, reliability, safety mindset, road knowledge.
- *Next doors:* Local/specialist driving. Fleet and logistics coordinator. Autonomous truck terminal operator. Diesel/EV heavy vehicle mechanic. Heavy equipment operator. Dispatcher.
- *Learn:* Logistics software. EV and heavy vehicle mechanics. Additional licences (dangerous goods, heavy machinery).
- *First step:* Price one additional licence or course this month.

**Taxi / rideshare driver**
- *Strengths:* Local knowledge, customer service.
- *Next doors:* Patient/disability transport. Specialist delivery. Trades apprenticeship. Logistics.
- *Learn:* Relevant licences. A trade.
- *First step:* Talk to one person doing each of those jobs about how they got in.

**Warehouse worker**
- *Strengths:* Physical work, logistics, safety.
- *Next doors:* Robotics/automation technician. Maintenance. Forklift/heavy equipment. Logistics coordinator.
- *Learn:* Mechatronics or maintenance certificate. Automation basics.
- *First step:* Ask your employer whether they fund technician training — many automated warehouses do.

**Factory production worker**
- *Strengths:* Process discipline, safety, technical familiarity.
- *Next doors:* Maintenance technician. Electrical apprenticeship. Quality control. Robot operator.
- *Learn:* Electrical or mechanical trade qualification.
- *First step:* Speak to your site's maintenance team about how they got their roles.

### ALREADY IN A TRADE

**Any trade**
- *Next doors (growth areas):* Electrical — data centres, solar, batteries, EV charging. HVAC — data centre cooling. Smart home installation. Trainer/assessor for new apprentices. Running a bigger business with AI-powered admin.
- *Learn:* The specialisation. AI business tools (Chapter 14).
- *First step:* Book one specialisation course.

### Proof Over Paper

There's one principle that applies to every door on this map.

In a world where AI can write a flawless CV, a perfect cover letter and a professional-sounding LinkedIn profile for anyone in thirty seconds, **paper claims are losing value.**

What's gaining value is **proof**.

- A portfolio of real work.
- A project people can see.
- A result you can point to: "I set up this system and it saved the team six hours a week."
- A reference from someone the employer trusts.
- A small business with real customers.
- A published article, a working app, a before-and-after.

Certificates still matter for some things, particularly licensed professions and trades. But for most career changes, **one real project is worth three online certificates.**

So when you're choosing your next door, don't only ask "what course should I do?" Ask: **"What can I build, fix, help with or produce in the next three months that proves I can do this?"**

---

### The three-question test before you spend money on training

Before you pay for any course, ask:

1. **Do people in the job I want actually list this qualification in job ads?** (Check ten real job listings.)
2. **Can I learn the first 20% for free** to test whether I even like it? (Usually yes.)
3. **What will I be able to *show* at the end?** (If the answer is "a certificate" and nothing else, be cautious.)

---

> ### DO THIS NOW
>
> **1. Find your row on the map.** Circle two "next doors" that interest you. (5 minutes)
>
> **2. Look up ten real job listings** for one of those doors, in your area. Write down the skills that appear most often. (30 minutes)
>
> **3. Design a proof project.** Something you could do in the next 90 days that would prove you can do that job. Write it in one sentence. (10 minutes)
>
> **4. Find one person doing that job** — via LinkedIn, a friend of a friend, a local business — and ask for fifteen minutes of their time. Most people say yes. (One message)
