## A Note Before We Start

I'm going to make you a promise, and then I'm going to ask you for something.

The promise is this: I will not lie to you in this book.

That sounds like a low bar. It isn't. Almost everything you've heard about artificial intelligence has been shouted at you by someone who wanted something. Tech companies want your subscription. Influencers want your clicks. Doom merchants want your fear, because fear keeps you watching. Even the sensible people on the news want a headline that will survive the scroll.

So you've ended up where most people are. Half-convinced it's all hype. Half-terrified it isn't. Unsure which half to trust.

I can't fix that by telling you to calm down. And I can't fix it by telling you to panic. What I can do is show you the evidence, tell you plainly how solid it is, and then hand you something to *do* about it.

That's the promise.

Here's what I'm asking for: your patience with uncertainty.

Nobody knows exactly what the next five years hold. Not the people building these systems. Not the economists. Not me. Anyone who tells you precisely what 2031 looks like is selling something. But "nobody knows exactly" isn't the same as "nobody knows anything." We know a lot. We can see which direction the wind is blowing and roughly how hard. We can prepare for a storm without knowing the exact minute it arrives.

So throughout this book, I'm going to label things the way a good weather forecaster would.

---

### The Four Forecast Labels

**NOW** — This is happening. It's documented. I'll tell you where the evidence comes from.

**LIKELY** — Strong evidence or broad agreement among serious people points this way. Not certain. Worth planning around.

**POSSIBLE** — Credible people argue for it, but the evidence is thin, or experts genuinely disagree. Worth knowing about. Not worth losing sleep over.

**WILD CARD** — It could happen. Nobody can reliably predict whether or when. I'll mention these so you're not blindsided, and then I'll move on.

When you see a number in this book, it came from somewhere real, and the notes at the end of each chapter tell you where. When I'm guessing, I'll say I'm guessing. When the experts are fighting, I'll show you both sides of the fight.

If you only remember one thing from this page, make it this:

> **You can't control how fast AI moves. You can control how ready you are when it arrives.**

---

### How to read this book

You don't have to read it in order. It's built so you can jump to the part that's keeping you up at night.

But there are three chapters I'd ask everyone to read, whatever else they skip:

- **Chapter 1** — because it shows you what's already happening, quietly, right now.
- **Chapter 6** — because it gives you a way to look at your own job honestly, in about twenty minutes.
- **Chapter 16** — because it could stop someone you love from being robbed.

Every chapter ends with a short box called **DO THIS NOW**. Three to five things. None of them takes long. Most take under twenty minutes. If you do nothing else with this book but tick those boxes, you'll be further ahead than most people you know.

That's not a sales line. It's just maths. Most people aren't doing anything at all.
