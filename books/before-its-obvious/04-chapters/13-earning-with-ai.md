# Chapter 13
## Earning With AI: From $0, $100 and $1,000

Let me start by making you a promise about this chapter. It's the chapter most likely to contain rubbish in other AI books, so I want to be clear.

**There is no secret AI money machine.**

There's no prompt that prints cash. There's no "passive income system" that makes $10,000 a month while you sleep, set up in a weekend. If someone is selling you one, they're making their money from *you*, not from the system.

What there *is*, right now, is a real and unusual opportunity. And it's much more ordinary, and much more achievable, than the hype.

Here it is:

> **Most businesses and most people have no idea how to use AI well. You can learn it faster than they can. And people will pay you to help them.**

That's it. That's the opportunity. It's not glamorous. It works.

Remember the MIT study from Chapter 1 — the one suggesting that the vast majority of corporate AI pilots weren't producing measurable results? That's not just a story about hype. It's a story about a *gap*. Businesses are spending money on AI and not getting value, because they don't know how to fit it into how they actually work.

Every gap is a job.

---

### The three kinds of AI income

Almost every realistic way to earn with AI falls into one of three buckets.

**1. AI-Boosted Services** — You do a service people already pay for, but AI lets you do it faster, better or cheaper. You keep more of the money or serve more people.
*Examples: writing, bookkeeping, marketing, design, virtual assistance, tutoring, research, translation-with-review, cleaning business run more efficiently.*

**2. AI Setup & Help** — You help businesses or people use AI themselves. You're the bridge.
*Examples: setting up AI tools for a local business; training staff; building simple automations; teaching older people to use AI safely; helping families with AI safety (Chapter 18).*

**3. Human-Premium Services** — Things people will pay *more* for because a real human does them. As AI makes everything else cheap, these become relatively more valuable. (Chapter 21.)
*Examples: personal training, coaching, handmade goods, local tours, in-person classes, trades, care, events, experiences.*

A fourth bucket — **digital products** (e-books, templates, courses, printables) — also exists, but I want to be honest: it's become much more crowded because AI makes it easy for everyone to produce them. It can work, especially if you have real expertise or an audience. It's rarely the fastest path for beginners.

## Starting with $0

You have: a phone or computer, internet access, free AI tools, and your own time and skills.

### Path A: The Local Business AI Helper
**What it is:** Help small local businesses — cafés, hairdressers, tradies, gyms, shops — with the online stuff they hate. Writing their Google Business profile. Replying to reviews. Writing social media posts. Rewriting their website text. Setting up simple AI tools.

**Why it works:** Small business owners are time-poor and often intimidated by technology. They don't want to learn AI. They want someone they trust to sort it out.

**How to start:**
1. Pick three businesses you already use and like.
2. Do something useful for one of them for free: rewrite their Google Business description, draft three weeks of social posts, or write polished replies to their last ten reviews.
3. Show them. Ask if it's useful. Ask if they'd pay you to keep doing it.
4. Use that as your first example and testimonial.

**Realistic earnings:** Many people doing this start with small monthly retainers per business. It grows as you add clients and add services. Rates vary enormously by location — check what local marketing freelancers charge.

**Skills needed:** Basic AI use (Chapter 14), friendliness, reliability.

### Path B: AI-Boosted Freelancing in a Skill You Already Have
**What it is:** Take something you're already good at — writing, admin, organising, research, spreadsheets, customer service — and offer it as a freelance service, using AI to deliver faster.

**How to start:** Offer it first to people you know, then local businesses, then online platforms. Be honest that you use AI tools; you're selling results, reliability and judgement.

**Warning:** Online freelance platforms have been flooded with low-priced AI-generated work. Competing on price there is a race to the bottom. Compete on *reliability, specialism and relationships* instead.

### Path C: Teach What You Just Learned
**What it is:** Once you've done the 30-day challenge in Chapter 15, you'll know more about practical AI than most people you know. Run a small, paid workshop — at a community centre, a library, for a local business group, for retirees.

**Why it works:** There's enormous demand for patient, human, jargon-free AI teaching, especially among older people and small business owners. The Stanford AI Index found that US adoption of generative AI lags behind many other countries. Most people still don't really know how to use it.

**How to start:** Run your first session free for friends and family. Collect feedback. Then offer it to a community group for a small fee per person.

## Starting with $100

You have: $100 to invest in tools, a simple website or a bit of advertising.

### Path D: Productised AI Service
**What it is:** Package one specific service with a clear price. Not "I do marketing." But: *"I'll set up your Google Business profile and write a month of posts for $X."* Or: *"I'll turn your voice memos into professional quotes and invoice templates for $X."*

**Spend the $100 on:** A simple one-page website or booking page. A paid AI subscription so you're using the best tools. A few printed flyers or a small local ad.

**Why it works:** A clear offer with a clear price is far easier to buy than a vague one.

### Path E: The Niche Digital Product (done properly)
**What it is:** A useful template, guide or toolkit for a specific group you understand. For example: a pack of AI prompts and templates for real estate agents; a quoting template kit for plumbers; a meal-planning system for busy parents with a child who has allergies.

**The honest bit:** This only works if (a) you really understand the group's problem, and (b) you can reach them — through your network, a community, social media or an existing audience. The product is the easy part now. Getting it in front of people is the hard part.

## Starting with $1,000

You have: $1,000 to invest, and you're prepared to put in real hours.

### Path F: AI Implementation for Small Businesses
**What it is:** A step up from Path A. You help businesses set up AI properly in their operations: an AI assistant on their website that answers questions and books appointments; automations connecting their email, calendar and accounting; staff training; ongoing support.

**Spend the $1,000 on:** Learning (good courses on the automation tools you'll use — many are cheap), paid tools for testing, a professional website, basic business setup (insurance, registration where required).

**Why it works:** Businesses will pay meaningful money for someone who saves them staff time. If you can show that your setup saves a business ten hours a week, that's easy maths for them.

**Skills needed:** Chapter 14's automation section, plus patience, clear communication and reliability. You don't need to be a programmer. You need to be the person who figures things out and explains them clearly.

### Path G: A Small AI-Powered Business in an Old Industry
**What it is:** Start a traditional service business — cleaning, lawn care, handyman, pet care, bookkeeping, tutoring — and run it with AI from day one: AI for marketing, booking, customer messages, quotes, scheduling and admin.

**Why it works:** You compete on the thing customers care about (good, reliable work) while spending far less time and money on admin than older competitors. The work itself is Anchored — human, physical, local. The business side is AI-boosted.

## Starting with existing professional skills

If you're an accountant, lawyer, nurse, teacher, engineer, HR professional, marketer or any other experienced professional:

**Path H: The Fractional Expert**
Offer your expertise part-time to several small businesses or clients who can't afford a full-time person. AI handles much of the grunt work, so you can serve more clients at a lower price. A bookkeeper becomes a part-time finance director for six small businesses. An HR professional becomes the part-time people advisor for five growing companies.

**Path I: The AI Bridge in Your Profession**
Your profession needs people who understand both the professional work and AI. Consult, train, write guides, or help firms in your field adopt AI safely and legally. A nurse who understands AI documentation tools. A lawyer who helps small firms adopt AI research tools without getting burned. A teacher who trains other teachers.

## Starting with trade skills

**Path J: Run a Tighter Trade Business**
Covered in Chapter 9. The biggest near-term financial win for most tradies isn't a new income stream. It's getting ten hours of admin back per week and using it for paid work.

**Path K: Specialise Into the Boom**
Electrical, HVAC, solar and battery installation, EV chargers, data centre work. Specialisation lifts your rate.

**Path L: Teach Your Trade**
As people move into trades from other work, demand for training grows. Mentoring, assessing apprentices or making practical how-to content can become a second income.

## Starting without a university degree

Everything in this chapter is open to you. None of it requires a degree. What it requires is:

- learning the tools (Chapter 14 and 15 — free),
- reliability (turning up, doing what you said, when you said),
- proof (one real example of your work, then another),
- and courage (asking the first person if they'd pay).

In a world where AI can make anyone *sound* qualified, the people who stand out are the ones who've actually *done* the thing. That's good news if you don't have letters after your name.

---

## How to spot the AI money scams

Because this area is full of them, here's your checklist. Be very suspicious if someone:

☐ Promises specific large earnings ("$10k/month with ChatGPT").
☐ Shows screenshots of income as proof.
☐ Says it's "passive" or "automated" and requires no skill or effort.
☐ Uses urgency ("only 3 spots left", "price goes up tonight").
☐ Wants you to recruit others.
☐ Offers an "AI trading bot" that guarantees returns.
☐ Asks you to pay in cryptocurrency or gift cards.
☐ Won't explain exactly what you'll be doing to earn money.

Three or more ticks: walk away.

The honest truth about earning with AI is that it looks a lot like earning without AI. You find people with a problem. You solve it well. They pay you. AI just lets you solve it faster, better, or for more people.

### Which path should you choose?

If you're paralysed by options, use this rule:

> **Start with the path closest to what you already know and who you already know.**

The fastest first dollar almost always comes from someone who already trusts you, for help with something you're already good at, done faster with AI.
