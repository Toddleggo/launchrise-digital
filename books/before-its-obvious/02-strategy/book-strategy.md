> **The calm, honest, practical plan for protecting your job, your money and your family through the next five years of AI — written for people who don't work in tech.**

| Theory | Checklists, scripts, 30-day challenge, scorecard |

**Core emotional line (repeat, never preach):**
*You can't control how fast AI moves. You can control how ready you are when it arrives.*

### Recommendation

**BEFORE IT'S OBVIOUS**
*What to Do Now to Protect Your Job, Your Money and Your Family in the Next Five Years of AI*

Why it wins:
- **True.** The research literally shows it: the entry-level hiring gap was measurable in payroll data a year before most people noticed.
- **Curiosity + urgency** without a single overclaim.
- **Identity:** the reader becomes "someone who saw it before it was obvious."
- **Built-in viral line:** *"By the time it's obvious, it's too late to be early."*

Back-up: **THE HEAD START** (softer, gift-friendly).
Action item before publishing: check Amazon/KDP for exact-title conflicts.

## 4. Named frameworks (the "shareable spine")

Each one = a chapter anchor, an infographic, and a 30-second video.

1. **The Four Forecast Labels** — NOW / LIKELY / POSSIBLE / WILD CARD. Used on every major claim in the book.
2. **The Doubling Clock** — the length of work AI can do on its own has been doubling every ~4–7 months.
3. **The Jagged Edge** — AI is genius and idiot at the same time; the danger is not knowing which one you're talking to.
4. **The Hiring Freeze Nobody Announced** — disruption shows up as jobs that never get posted, not people who get fired.
5. **The Task Test (A-A-A)** — split your job into tasks: **Automate** (AI can do it), **Assist** (AI makes you faster), **Anchor** (needs a human: hands, trust, accountability, judgement).
6. **The Five Moats** — what stays hard to automate: *Hands, Heart, Trust, Taste, Accountability.*
7. **Don't Out-AI the AI. Direct It.** — The Director Principle.
8. **The Income Tripod** — main income + one skill-based side income + a cash cushion. Two legs wobble; three stand.
9. **The Human Premium** — as AI makes things cheap, people pay more for the real, local, trusted human.
10. **Proof Over Paper** — portfolios, results and references beat certificates in a world where anyone can generate a CV.
11. **The Family Safe Word + Hang Up, Call Back** — the two-minute defence that beats a $25M deepfake.
12. **The Lazy/Smart Fork** — the same AI can make a child brighter or duller; design decides which.
13. **The 30-Day AI Reskill Challenge.**
14. **The Family AI Preparedness Plan.**
15. **The AI Resilience Scorecard** (10 areas, scored 0–5).

## 6. Voice rules (the "no-AI-smell" rulebook)

1. **Specific beats general.** A Hong Kong finance worker. Fifteen transfers. Every face on the call was fake. Never "scams are rising."
2. **Short. Then shorter.** Paragraphs of 1–4 lines. Vary sentence length on purpose.
3. **Talk to one person.** "You." Occasionally "we." Never "individuals" or "stakeholders."
4. **Admit uncertainty out loud.** "I don't know. Neither does anyone else. Here's how to plan anyway."
5. **Warmth without syrup.** Kindness through honesty and practical help, not adjectives.
6. **Banned words/phrases:** delve, landscape, leverage, unlock, harness, tapestry, navigate the complexities, in today's rapidly evolving world, game-changer, revolutionise, it's important to note, in conclusion, furthermore, moreover, crucial, embark, seamless, robust.
7. **No tidy triplets everywhere.** Real writers break patterns.
8. **Stories labelled honestly.** Real documented cases = named and cited. Illustrative scenarios = clearly "Imagine…" or composites. **No invented interviews, quotes or statistics — ever.**
9. **Every chapter ends with "DO THIS NOW"** (3–5 actions, under 20 minutes each) and research notes.
10. **Author's own stories:** placeholders marked `[AUTHOR STORY]` for real personal experiences only the author can supply. This is what makes it undeniably human.

## 7. Voice sample (proposed opening of Chapter 1)

> Nobody got fired.
>
> That's the part people miss.
>
> When we imagine machines taking jobs, we picture something loud. A factory floor going quiet. A boss with a cardboard box. A news crew outside the gates.
>
> What's actually happening is quieter than that, and in some ways worse, because you can't see it.
>
> A woman in accounts leaves to have a baby, and nobody replaces her. Her work gets split between two colleagues and "the new system." A law firm that used to hire eight graduates hires three. A marketing agency stops posting junior roles at all. No announcement. No layoffs. Just a door that used to be open, now quietly shut.
>
> In August 2026, economists at Stanford went through payroll records covering millions of American workers. They found no sign of a jobs collapse. Most people's work looked the way it always had.
>
> Except for one group.
>
> Young people, aged 22 to 25, in the jobs most exposed to AI — software, customer service, certain kinds of office work — were employed at a rate about 19% lower than you'd expect. The gap had been 15% a year earlier. It was growing.
>
> Older, experienced workers in the same jobs? Fine. So far.
>
> The researchers were careful. They said the pattern doesn't prove AI caused it. They're right to be careful, and I'll be careful too. But I want you to notice *how* it happened: not through firings, through hirings that never happened.
>
> That's what change looks like before it's obvious.
>
> And that's why I wrote this book. Not to scare you. You've had enough of that. I wrote it because by the time something is obvious to everyone, the cheapest, easiest moment to prepare for it has already passed.
>
> That moment is now. And you've got more time than you fear — but less than you'd like.
>
> Let's use it well.
